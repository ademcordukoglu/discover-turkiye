Eastern Anatolia

Ani

The haunting ruins of a medieval Armenian capital — the "City of 1,001 Churches" — scattered across a grassy steppe on the very edge of Türkiye. A thousand years ago it rivalled Constantinople; today you may have it almost to yourself.

Where
45 km from Kars, Northeast
Best time
May–Sep; winter is stark but beautiful
Getting there
45 km east of Kars (KSY airport, flights from Istanbul and Ankara); taxis and small tours make the run, or drive the empty steppe roads yourself.
Eat & drink
Kars is Türkiye's cheese capital — aged gravyer, honey from mountain meadows, and goose roasted the local way in winter.
Tip
Allow at least half a day and wear good shoes — the site is vast. Kars is famous for honey and gravyer cheese.

The story

Around the year 1000, Ani held perhaps 100,000 people and traded on the Silk Road as an equal of Constantinople and Cairo. Capital of the Bagratid Armenian kingdom, it was so studded with sanctuaries that chroniclers called it the City of 1,001 Churches. Then came conquest — Byzantine, Seljuk, Mongol — an earthquake in 1319, shifting trade routes, and finally silence. What remains stands alone on a grass plateau ringed by the Akhurian river gorge, on the very border with Armenia.

You wander from ruin to ruin with skylarks for company: the cathedral whose architect also repaired Hagia Sophia's dome, the round church of St Gregory frescoed floor to ceiling, a Seljuk palace, a mosque with a view over the gorge, fragments of a 1,600-year-old bridge. Few places in the world feel so charged with vanished greatness. Take water, sun cover and at least half a day; combine it with Kars' Baltic-style stone architecture and famous honey-and-cheese breakfasts.

01

Marmara & Istanbul

Two continents, three empires — and the walls of Troy

UNESCO

Hagia Sophia

Built in 537 AD as the greatest cathedral of Byzantium, later an imperial mosque — for almost a thousand years this was the largest enclosed space on Earth. Its floating golden dome and layered history make it Türkiye’s single most famous building.

Where: Sultanahmet, Istanbul
Best time: Apr–Jun & Sep–Nov; go right at opening
Tip: Foreign visitors tour the upper gallery (ticket ≈ €25); closed to tourists during prayer times.
UNESCO

Blue Mosque & Sultanahmet

The Sultan Ahmed Mosque, crowned by six minarets and lined with 20,000 handmade İznik tiles, faces Hagia Sophia across a rose garden. Around it lies the old imperial quarter: Topkapı Palace, the Basilica Cistern and the Grand Bazaar’s 4,000 shops.

Where: Sultanahmet, Istanbul
Best time: Year-round; dusk for the best photos
Tip: Entry is free, outside the five daily prayers. Dress modestly; scarves are provided at the door.

Galata & the Bosphorus

Cross the Galata Bridge past anglers and fish sandwiches, climb the 14th-century Genoese tower for a 360° panorama, then take a ferry up the strait that separates Europe from Asia — the cheapest world-class cruise anywhere.

Where: Karaköy / Beyoğlu, Istanbul
Best time: Year-round; sunset ferry is magical
Tip: Use an Istanbulkart for ferries, trams and metro — public ferries beat pricey tourist cruises.
UNESCO

Troy

The legendary city of Homer’s Iliad, where archaeologists found nine cities stacked over 4,000 years. Walk the ancient walls, pose with the wooden horse, then dive deeper at the striking modern Troy Museum next door.

Where: Near Çanakkale, Dardanelles
Best time: Apr–Jun & Sep–Oct
Tip: Combine with the moving Gallipoli battlefields across the strait for a full day of history.
02

Aegean Coast

Marble cities of antiquity, thermal terraces and whitewashed harbours

UNESCO

Pamukkale & Hierapolis

The "Cotton Castle": a hillside of dazzling white travertine terraces filled with warm, mineral-rich water. On the plateau above sit the ruins of Roman Hierapolis, where you can swim among sunken ancient columns in Cleopatra’s Pool.

Where: Near Denizli, Aegean region
Best time: Early morning or golden hour; avoid midday heat
Tip: Shoes come off on the terraces — bring a bag for them and non-slip confidence; the stone can be slick.
UNESCO

Ephesus

The best-preserved classical city of the eastern Mediterranean. Walk marble streets past the two-storey Library of Celsus, terraced Roman houses with intact frescoes, and a great theatre that once seated 25,000 spectators.

Where: Selçuk, near İzmir
Best time: Mar–May & Sep–Nov; first hour after opening
Tip: Hire a guide or audio guide — the stones come alive. Don’t skip the Terrace Houses (separate ticket).

Bodrum

Whitewashed houses and bougainvillea spill down to a bay guarded by the Castle of St. Peter, home to a world-class Museum of Underwater Archaeology. Ancient Halicarnassus’ Mausoleum — one of the Seven Wonders — once stood here.

Where: Bodrum Peninsula, Aegean
Best time: May–Oct; liveliest in high summer
Tip: Charter a traditional gulet for a "Blue Cruise" along the coast — even a one-day trip is worth it.
03

Mediterranean — the Turquoise Coast

Pine-fringed lagoons, Lycian ruins and 300 days of sunshine

Ölüdeniz & Fethiye

A still, turquoise lagoon curling around a white spit of sand beneath pine-covered mountains — Türkiye’s most photographed beach. Tandem paragliders launch from 1,900 m Babadağ and spiral down over the bay; the Lycian Way starts nearby.

Where: Fethiye, Turquoise Coast
Best time: May–Oct; June & September are ideal
Tip: Book paragliding for morning slots (calmer air) and take a boat day-trip to Butterfly Valley.

Antalya

The capital of the Turkish Riviera: an Ottoman old town (Kaleiçi) wrapped around a Roman harbour, waterfalls that plunge straight into the sea, and long beaches backed by the Taurus mountains. Perge, Aspendos and Side are an easy drive away.

Where: Mediterranean coast
Best time: Apr–Jun & Sep–Nov; swimming into November
Tip: See the Lower Düden waterfall from a boat, and catch a summer concert in the Roman theatre of Aspendos.

Side & Aspendos

A Greco-Roman port town on a small peninsula, where the marble columns of the Temple of Apollo frame the sunset over the sea. Nearby Aspendos preserves the best-kept Roman theatre in the world — still hosting performances.

Where: Manavgat, east of Antalya
Best time: Apr–Jun & Sep–Nov; sunset at the temple
Tip: The ruins are woven through the modern village — stroll the colonnaded street down to the old harbour.
04

Central Anatolia

Fairy chimneys, underground cities and the home of the whirling dervishes

UNESCO

Cappadocia

A surreal volcanic landscape of fairy chimneys, hidden Byzantine cave churches and entire underground cities. At dawn, more than a hundred hot-air balloons drift over the valleys of Göreme — one of the world’s great travel spectacles.

Where: Göreme / Nevşehir, Central Anatolia
Best time: Apr–Jun & Sep–Nov for flights
Tip: Book balloons ahead and allow 2–3 mornings — flights are cancelled in wind. Stay in a cave hotel.

Konya & the Mevlâna

The spiritual heart of Türkiye, where the 13th-century Sufi poet Rumi taught and is buried beneath a turquoise-tiled dome. His mausoleum-museum and the mesmerising sema ceremony of the whirling dervishes draw pilgrims from every faith.

Where: Konya, Central Anatolia
Best time: Year-round; Rumi festival in December
Tip: See an authentic sema ceremony (Saturdays at the Mevlâna Cultural Centre) and try etli ekmek, Konya’s metre-long flatbread.
05

Black Sea

Cliff monasteries, tea gardens and perfectly preserved Ottoman towns

Sumela Monastery

A Greek Orthodox monastery founded in the 4th century, clinging impossibly to a sheer cliff 300 metres above a misty forest valley. Inside, restored chapels are covered in vivid frescoes; outside, the Black Sea’s greenest scenery.

Where: Altındere Valley, near Trabzon
Best time: May–Oct; often shrouded in atmospheric mist
Tip: Combine with the alpine lake of Uzungöl and a çay (tea) stop — this region grows all of Türkiye’s tea.
UNESCO

Safranbolu

A perfectly preserved Ottoman merchant town of timber-framed mansions, cobbled lanes, a 17th-century caravanserai and artisan bazaars — named after the saffron that still grows in the surrounding fields.

Where: Karabük, ~2h from Ankara
Best time: Apr–Oct; lovely under winter snow too
Tip: Sleep in a konak (Ottoman mansion), try saffron lokum, and climb to Hıdırlık hill for the rooftop view.

Uzungöl

A mirror-still mountain lake at 1,090 m, with a slender-minaret mosque at the water’s edge and spruce forest climbing into the mist — the Black Sea’s postcard view and a cool escape even in high summer.

Where: Çaykara, Trabzon
Best time: May–Oct; misty mornings are the magic
Tip: Sleep over — the lake is quietest before the day-trip buses arrive and after they leave.
06

Eastern Anatolia

High plateaus, a ghost capital and church islands on a soda lake

UNESCO

Ani

The haunting ruins of a medieval Armenian capital — the "City of 1,001 Churches" — scattered across a grassy steppe on the very edge of Türkiye. A thousand years ago it rivalled Constantinople; today you may have it almost to yourself.

Where: 45 km from Kars, Northeast
Best time: May–Sep; winter is stark but beautiful
Tip: Allow at least half a day and wear good shoes — the site is vast. Kars is famous for honey and gravyer cheese.

Lake Van & Akdamar

Türkiye’s largest lake — a vast, soda-blue inland sea ringed by volcanoes. A short boat ride brings you to Akdamar Island and its 10th-century Armenian Church of the Holy Cross, carved with vivid biblical reliefs.

Where: Van, far East
Best time: May–Sep; almond blossom in April
Tip: Start the day with a legendary Van breakfast — dozens of little plates of cheeses, honey and kaymak.
07

Southeastern Anatolia

Mesopotamia: the world’s first temple and cities older than history

UNESCO

Göbekli Tepe

The oldest known monumental temple on Earth, raised by hunter-gatherers around 9600 BC — some 6,000 years before Stonehenge. Rings of T-shaped megaliths carved with foxes, vultures and scorpions are rewriting the story of civilisation.

Where: Near Şanlıurfa, Southeast
Best time: Mar–May & Oct–Nov; summers are scorching
Tip: Pair it with Şanlıurfa’s superb Archaeology Museum and the carp pools of Balıklıgöl in the old town.
UNESCO

Mount Nemrut

On a 2,134 m summit, a forgotten king of Commagene built his tomb-sanctuary guarded by colossal stone heads of gods and eagles. At sunrise and sunset the toppled statues glow orange above a sea of mountains — pure otherworldliness.

Where: Adıyaman, Eastern Taurus
Best time: May–Oct (road closed by snow in winter)
Tip: Go for sunrise, bring warm layers even in August — the summit wind is icy. Base yourself in Kahta or Karadut.

Mardin

Honey-coloured stone houses cascade down a hillside above the endless Mesopotamian plain. Minarets, ancient Syriac monasteries, silver filigree workshops and courtyard cafés make this Türkiye’s most atmospheric southeastern town.

Where: Southeastern Anatolia
Best time: Mar–May & Sep–Nov
Tip: Visit Deyrulzafaran (Saffron) Monastery at golden hour and stay in a restored stone mansion hotel.

Good to know

Practical tips for your trip

A few things that make travelling in Türkiye easier — from visas and money to mosque etiquette.

Visas & entry

Many nationalities enter visa-free or with a quick e-Visa from the official site evisa.gov.tr. Check your passport’s rules before booking.

Money

The currency is the Turkish lira (₺). Cards are accepted almost everywhere, but keep small cash for bazaars, taxis and tips — and haggling in bazaars is expected.

Mosque etiquette

Mosques are free to visit outside prayer times. Shoulders and knees covered, shoes off at the door, headscarves for women (usually provided). Keep voices low.

Getting around

Distances are big: cheap domestic flights (THY, Pegasus, AJet) link all regions, intercity buses are excellent, and high-speed trains connect Istanbul, Ankara and Konya.

Museum Pass

The official Museum Pass Türkiye (muze.gov.tr) covers hundreds of state-run sites — Ephesus, Göbekli Tepe, Topkapı and more — and lets you skip ticket queues.

Food & tea culture

Say yes to çay — tea is the language of hospitality. Try a full Turkish breakfast, street simit, pide, meze by the sea and baklava with pistachio from Gaziantep.
